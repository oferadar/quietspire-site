Words for sad

Sad covers a lot of ground. These words divide it up: the quiet gloom of glum, the tears of lachrymose, the overdone misery of lugubrious, and the long, mournful complaint of a jeremiad.

dismay

dis-MAY, noun

A sinking mix of distress and disappointment.

“She read the repair estimate with growing dismay.”

gloomy

GLOO-mee, adjective

Dark, dim, or low in spirits.

“The gloomy forecast canceled the picnic before the rain did.”

glum

GLUHM, adjective

Quietly gloomy or dejected.

“The team sat glum after the demo crashed twice in a row.”

grim

GRIM, adjective

Serious, harsh, or without hope.

“The coach’s grim expression said more than the scoreboard.”

bleak

BLEEK, adjective

Offering little hope or comfort; cold and bare.

“The forecast was bleak: rain for the entire holiday.”

brood

BROOD, verb

To dwell gloomily on something.

“He brooded over the missed penalty for a full season.”

dismal

DIZ-muhl, adjective

Gloomy and depressing; disgracefully bad.

“The team’s dismal first half emptied half the stadium.”

forlorn

for-LORN, adjective

Sadly abandoned or lonely in appearance.

“A single forlorn mitten spent the winter on the fence post.”

somber

SOM-ber, adjective

Dark, serious, or gloomy in tone.

“The locker room was somber until someone found the bloopers reel.”

wistful

WIST-fuhl, adjective

Full of gentle, thoughtful longing.

“He went wistful at the smell of his grandmother’s spice cabinet.”

despondent

dih-SPON-duhnt, adjective

In low spirits from loss of hope.

“He was despondent for a week after the rejection, then rewrote it.”

lugubrious

luh-GOO-bree-uhs, adjective

Looking or sounding sad and dismal, often to an exaggerated degree.

“The basset hound’s lugubrious expression suggested a life of quiet tragedy.”

maudlin

MAWD-lin, adjective

Excessively sentimental.

“The toast turned maudlin after the second glass.”

jeremiad

jair-uh-MY-uhd, noun

A long, mournful complaint or lament.

“The group chat received his jeremiad on airline seating in four parts.”

lachrymose

LAK-ruh-mohs, adjective

Tearful; inclined to weep.

“The film’s lachrymose finale emptied the tissue aisle.”

saturnine

SAT-er-nahyn, adjective

Gloomy and brooding in temperament.

“The saturnine doorman never once returned a smile.”

threnody

THREN-uh-dee, noun

A song or poem of mourning.

“The bagpipes turned the drizzle into a threnody for the season.”
