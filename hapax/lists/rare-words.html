Rare words

English is full of words almost nobody uses, and some of them name things you have always noticed and never had a word for: the rumble of a stomach, a misheard song lyric, a person who stands and stares. These are the best of them.

defenestration

dee-fen-uh-STRAY-shuhn, noun

The act of throwing someone or something out of a window.

“The pivot began with the ceremonial defenestration of the old roadmap.”

absquatulate

ab-SKWOCH-uh-layt, verb

To leave abruptly; to make off.

“The cat absquatulated the moment the carrier appeared.”

agelast

AJ-uh-last, noun

A person who never laughs.

“Even the office agelast cracked at the typo in the CEO’s memo.”

anfractuous

an-FRAK-choo-uhs, adjective

Full of windings and turnings; circuitous.

“The refund process was anfractuous by design.”

aposiopesis

ap-uh-sy-uh-PEE-sis, noun

Breaking off a sentence midway, leaving it unfinished.

“Her text was a single aposiopesis: ‘If you think for one second…’”

borborygmus

bor-buh-RIG-muhs, noun

The rumbling sound of a stomach.

“A solemn silence, broken only by one traitorous borborygmus.”

cachinnate

KAK-ih-nayt, verb

To laugh loudly and immoderately.

“Something in row three cachinnated through the entire eulogy scene.”

deipnosophist

dyp-NOS-uh-fist, noun

A masterful dinner-table conversationalist.

“Seat the deipnosophist mid-table and the party runs itself.”

funambulist

fyoo-NAM-byuh-list, noun

A tightrope walker.

“She budgets like a funambulist: steady, precise, no looking down.”

gallimaufry

gal-ih-MAW-free, noun

A confused jumble; a hodgepodge.

“The garage sale was a gallimaufry of three generations’ hobbies.”

gongoozler

gon-GOOZ-ler, noun

An idle spectator, especially one who stares at length.

“Every construction fence gathers its faithful gongoozlers.”

hebdomadal

heb-DOM-uh-duhl, adjective

Occurring weekly.

“The hebdomadal trash scramble happens every Sunday at eleven.”

illeism

IL-ee-iz-uhm, noun

The habit of referring to oneself in the third person.

“His illeism made the memo read like a royal decree.”

jentacular

jen-TAK-yuh-ler, adjective

Of or relating to breakfast.

“He guards his jentacular hour against all calendar invites.”

logomachy

loh-GOM-uh-kee, noun

A dispute about words rather than substance.

“The standards meeting collapsed into logomachy over ‘shall’ and ‘should.’”

lucubration

loo-kyoo-BRAY-shuhn, noun

Laborious study late into the night; its written product.

“The thesis owes its best chapter to one desperate week of lucubration.”

mondegreen

MON-dih-green, noun

A misheard song lyric or phrase.

“For years her favorite mondegreen was ‘hold me closer, Tony Danza.’”

mumpsimus

MUMP-sih-muhs, noun

A person who clings to an error even after correction.

“The style-guide mumpsimus still writes ‘alot,’ defiantly.”

omphaloskepsis

om-fuh-loh-SKEP-sis, noun

Navel-gazing; idle self-absorbed reflection.

“The retreat’s third day dissolved into group omphaloskepsis.”

opsimath

OP-sih-math, noun

A person who begins learning late in life.

“At seventy she became an opsimath of the cello, unembarrassed.”

perendinate

per-EN-dih-nayt, verb

To postpone until the day after tomorrow.

“He doesn’t procrastinate; he perendinates, with a calendar entry.”

quidnunc

KWID-nungk, noun

A busybody eager for the latest gossip.

“The building’s quidnunc knows the elevator schedule of every tenant.”

sprachgefühl

SHPRAHK-guh-fyool, noun

An intuitive feel for the natural idiom of a language.

“Grammar rules take you far; sprachgefühl writes the good sentence.”

tatterdemalion

tat-er-dih-MAYL-yuhn, noun

A person in ragged, tattered clothing.

“The scarecrow stood like a cheerful tatterdemalion among the rows.”

thaumaturgy

THAW-muh-tur-jee, noun

The working of wonders or miracles.

“Restoring the corrupted file struck the office as pure thaumaturgy.”

tmesis

TMEE-sis, noun

Splitting a word with another word inserted, as in “abso-bloody-lutely.”

“Her tmesis game peaks at ‘un-be-lievable’ during playoffs.”

ultracrepidarian

uhl-truh-krep-ih-DAIR-ee-uhn, noun

A person who opines confidently beyond their knowledge.

“Every comment section has its resident ultracrepidarian.”

velleity

vuh-LEE-ih-tee, noun

A wish too faint to prompt any action.

“Learning the cello remained a velleity, renewed each January.”

widdershins

WID-er-shinz, adverb

In a direction contrary to the sun’s course; counterclockwise.

“Superstitious, she stirs the batter widdershins on game days.”

zeugma

ZOOG-muh, noun

One word yoking two others in different senses, as in “she left in tears and a taxi.”

“He exited the meeting with his laptop and his dignity … a zeugma, barely.”
