Words for tired and sleepy

Words for the far end of the day: sleepy, weary, slowed down, dragging your feet, dulled, and the drifting state just before sleep.

drowsy

DROW-zee, adjective

Sleepy and slow to react.

“The warm train and soft seats made the commuters drowsy.”

sluggish

SLUHG-ish, adjective

Slow-moving; lacking energy.

“The app felt sluggish until the cache warmed up.”

weary

WEER-ee, adjective

Very tired, especially after long effort.

“The weary travelers cheered when the gate finally opened.”

trudge

TRUHJ, verb

To walk heavily, with effort or reluctance.

“We trudged the last mile in wet boots, saying nothing.”

lassitude

LAS-ih-tood, noun

A state of weary lack of energy.

“August lassitude settled over the office like a warm towel.”

somnolent

SOM-nuh-luhnt, adjective

Sleepy; inducing drowsiness.

“The somnolent hum of the seminar defeated the back row.”

soporific

sop-uh-RIF-ik, adjective

Causing sleep; extremely boring.

“The keynote was so soporific the ushers dimmed the lights out of mercy.”

hebetude

HEB-ih-tood, noun

Mental dullness or lethargy.

“The 3 p.m. hebetude lifted only when someone said ‘cake.’”

hypnagogic

hip-nuh-GOJ-ik, adjective

Of the drowsy state just before sleep.

“The solution arrived in a hypnagogic flash and was gone by morning.”

oneiric

oh-NY-rik, adjective

Of or resembling dreams.

“Jet lag gave the airport an oneiric shimmer at 4 a.m.”
