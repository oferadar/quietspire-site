Hapax press kit

One word a day, on your Lock Screen and Home Screen. The word is free, works offline and needs no account.

What it is

Hapax puts one hand-picked word on your iPhone Lock Screen and Home Screen every midnight: the word, its meaning, how to say it, and one honest example sentence. On Android it lives in a Home Screen widget and, if you turn it on, your lock screen wallpaper. The app behind the widget keeps every word you have met, a quiet spaced-repetition Practice, four difficulty levels from everyday words to ones even word lovers have never met, and ten themes.

What it does not have is the point. No ads, no account, no notifications, no tracking. The App Store privacy label reads "Data Not Collected" because there is nothing to collect. The words, the widgets and Practice all work offline.

The story

Hapax is built by one person under Quietspire LLC, a small independent studio in New Jersey. It exists because vocabulary apps became slot machines: video ads between flashcards and streak guilt. Hapax goes the other way. The word of the day, the widgets and Practice are free, with no ads. What pays for it is optional: one-time word packs, and the whole History by subscription or as part of The Whole Library.

A "hapax" is a word that appears exactly once in a text. That is how often you meet each word here: once, at its moment, remembered.
