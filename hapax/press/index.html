Hapax press kit

One word a day, on your Lock Screen. Free, offline, no account.

What it is

Hapax puts one hand-picked word on your iPhone Lock Screen and Home Screen every midnight: the word, its meaning, and one honest example sentence. The app behind the widget holds your full history, a quiet spaced-repetition practice mode, four difficulty levels from everyday to words even word lovers have never met, and nine typographic themes.

What it does not have is the point. No ads, no subscriptions, no account, no notifications, no tracking. The App Store privacy label reads "Data Not Collected" because there is nothing to collect. The whole app works offline and weighs less than a photo.

The story

Hapax is built by one person under Quietspire LLC, a small independent studio in New Jersey. It exists because vocabulary apps became slot machines: $40-a-year subscriptions, video ads between flashcards, streak guilt, and paywalls in front of features that used to be free. Hapax is the opposite bet: a beautiful, permanent, free utility for people who love words, funded later by optional one-time word packs and nothing else.

A "hapax" is a word that appears exactly once in a text. That is how often you meet each word here: once, at its moment, remembered.
